# 00 · Balance Philosophy & Objectives

> Bible §1–3. See also [`../data/balance-targets.json`](../data/balance-targets.json).

## Central principle

**The player who scouts better, spends more efficiently, positions more intelligently, and adapts faster should usually win.**

Victory should **not** be determined primarily by: faction selection · one dominant build order · unscouted surprise units with no reaction window · unit spam · excessive defensive play · a single support power · random combat outcomes · purchased advantages · unavoidable superweapons · mechanical difficulty alone.

**Preserve faction personality.** Factions should not be identical tanks/economies/counters with different art. Each faction solves the same strategic problems a different way.

## Competitive objectives (launch)

The measurable targets and the warning-sign list live in [`balance-targets.json`](../data/balance-targets.json). These are **warning indicators, not proof** — a faction at exactly 50% can still be unhealthy if it has one mandatory strategy, is frustrating to fight, dominates beginners, performs poorly at high skill, relies on one overpowered unit, or wins mainly by surprise mechanics.

## Five layers of balance (all must be tested)

1. **Unit** — is each unit worth its cost and production time?
2. **Composition** — do mixed armies outperform simple spam under normal conditions?
3. **Faction** — does each faction have viable answers to every major threat?
4. **Map** — do terrain, resources, and routes favor one faction or spawn?
5. **Skill level** — is it fair for beginners, experienced, and elite players?

A unit can be balanced alone but oppressive when combined with another. A faction can be balanced on one map but dominant across maps with narrow chokes.

## The 20 final commandments

1. Never give one faction the best economy, mobility, and combat strength.
2. Every strength must create a weakness or opportunity cost.
3. Counters should be strong, but player control should still matter.
4. Economy must be vulnerable.
5. Expansion must require risk.
6. Defensive structures must delay armies, not replace them.
7. Artillery must be vulnerable when reached.
8. Aircraft must depend on infrastructure.
9. Stealth must have accessible detection.
10. Control-removing effects must be brief.
11. Superweapons must force action, not guarantee victory.
12. Epic units must require support.
13. Mixed armies should usually outperform spam.
14. Randomness must never decide important battles.
15. Maps must be tested as carefully as units.
16. Balance must be measured separately at different skill levels.
17. Fix readability before weakening an otherwise fair strategy.
18. Make small balance changes whenever possible.
19. Preserve faction identity.
20. Better decisions should defeat easier strategies over time.

> The ideal is **not** a game where everything feels equal — it's one where every faction feels powerful, feels unfair in its own specialized situation, and every one of those strengths has a discoverable, practical, satisfying counter.
